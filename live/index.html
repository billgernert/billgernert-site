Live

Running evidence, kept public-safe

Live is the public operating view of the lab: one architecture page that shows the running shape of the estate, and one monitoring page that shows the public-safe status surface. It exists to show evidence from the running system without publishing the identifiers the system runs on.

Open the diagram Open the NOC

Two views, two jobs

Live is a hub because the two pages answer different questions. The diagram explains what is in the estate and how the major systems relate. The NOC answers the glance question: what is being measured right now, and where should a deeper look start.

  1. Diagram. A sanitized platform map: network segments, compute layers, core services, and the pages that explain them. It is about running shape, not private wiring. Open the diagram →
  2. NOC. A public-safe Grafana entry point: one overview hub, curated drilldowns, and a rule that no data never reads as green. Open the NOC →

The sanitization boundary is part of the design

The public boundary is enforced on purpose. The diagram keeps the shape and removes the identifiers that would turn it into a map of the private estate. The NOC keeps the operating signals that are safe to publish and leaves out hostnames, IPs, ports, datasource URLs, query parameters, app names, and private dashboard identifiers. That is not apology copy. It is the same engineering rule as any other boundary: publish the evidence, keep the control plane private.

The rule also keeps the claims honest. If a subsystem is designed and not deployed yet, it stays described as designed on its own page and does not get promoted here as running proof.

What Live will not imply

  • A designed subsystem is not shown here as live evidence until it is deployed or measured.
  • The NOC keeps the "no data never green" rule explicit, so an unmeasured surface stays visibly unmeasured.
  • Deeper design, trade-offs, and planned work stay on the explanatory pages; Live points at current structure and current signal.

Follow the evidence back to the system

Use the diagram when the question is structure. Use the NOC when the question is current signal. From either page, follow through to the subsystem writeups for the design, the limits, and the work that is still planned rather than running.
