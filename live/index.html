Three views, 3 jobs

Live is a hub because the three pages answer different questions. The map follows current health from the whole estate to an individual component. The diagram explains the intended structure. The NOC answers the glance question: what is being measured right now, and where should a deeper look start.

  1. Live map. The same layers and display names as the private operating atlas, with addresses, raw alerts, exact capacity, and administration links removed before publication. Open the live map →
  2. Diagram. A sanitized platform map: network segments, compute layers, core services, and the pages that explain them. It is about running shape, not private wiring. Open the diagram →
  3. NOC. The live Grafana boards: one overview hub, curated drilldowns, and a rule that no data never reads as green. Open the NOC →

What is left out, and why

The live map keeps the reviewed display names because they are the vocabulary that makes its drill-down useful. It replaces internal IDs, addresses, domain suffixes, raw alerts, exact capacity, and administration links before the feed leaves the lab. The diagram and NOC apply their own narrower publication boundaries. The NOC page explains exactly what that costs and what it keeps. Two other rules hold here. A subsystem that is designed but not deployed stays described as designed on its own page and is never promoted to running proof. And a surface that is not measured stays visibly unmeasured, because a green tile with no data behind it is the one thing this page must never show.

Follow the evidence back to the system

Use the map when the question is where a current state comes from. Use the diagram when the question is structure. Use the NOC when the question is current signal. From any view, follow through to the subsystem writeups for the design, limits, and planned work.
