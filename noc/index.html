Monitoring

The NOC hub

These are the live dashboards. Grafana serves them from the running lab, and every tile and panel on this page is interactive: click a tile and the domain's own dashboard opens, drag across a graph and it zooms, hover a series and it tells you what it is. Nothing here is a screenshot.

They are also the plain versions. The dashboards I run the lab from show hostnames, addresses, alert text and the query behind every panel. These show the shape and the state, under stable aliases, because the public host serves anyone. That trade is deliberate, and the rest of this page explains what is in front of you and what has been left out.

Live status board

One screen you can glance at and know whether the estate is healthy. 1 tile per operational view, each reduced to one of four states before it leaves the monitoring system: HEALTHY, DEGRADED, CRITICAL, or NO SIGNAL. Every tile is a door. Click one and the domain's public overview opens, so the hub is the starting point and the detail is one click away.

The board updates on its own. The badge above it reports whether the public monitoring route answered on the last check and how long ago. If the route fails, the badge says so and the page keeps the last state it received rather than pretending.

The tiles, and what grey means

1 tile per operational view, each coloured by the worst severity in that view: green is healthy, amber and red escalate, and grey means no data. Grey is the honest one. Some domains are not fully wired yet, so their tiles are grey today, and the page says that plainly rather than hiding it. That matters because of a rule the hub enforces: a tile with no data must never render green. A green tile is a promise that the domain is healthy, and a domain you are not actually measuring cannot make that promise. So it stays grey until real data backs it, which is exactly the bug this rule exists to prevent: a monitoring screen that looks reassuring while measuring nothing.

The facts row

Under the tiles sit three supporting signals: current alert state, public web-console health, and backup freshness. Each is clickable, and each is backed by a real public data projection rather than a placeholder. Certificate horizon and secrets-service state are combined into the Certificates & PKI tile above, where they can be investigated together.

What you are not seeing

Before Grafana can query anything on the public host, recording rules rewrite the source series. Hostnames become service names. Guest IDs, pod names and namespaces become stable aliases. Addresses, hypervisor placement, management URLs, alert text and the editable query behind each panel never reach the public series at all. The internal dashboards carry all of that and are richer for it. The public ones give up the identifiers to keep the state, which is the part that proves the lab is running.

The public host itself has no login path for anyone. Everything except these dashboards, their data route and the health check goes to Cloudflare Access, where the only policy is block.

Generated, and it polices itself

The part a screenshot cannot show is the part that matters most: the hub is not arranged by hand, it is generated from the backlog, and the generator is the enforcement point. It fails the build if a domain is missing its tag or if a domain has no single primary dashboard to link to, a drift check in CI catches a hub that has fallen out of step, and a nightly job reconciles it against what Grafana is actually serving. That is what keeps the no-data-never-green rule and the 1-tile-per-domain shape true over time, rather than true on the day it was built. A hand-maintained wall drifts; a generated one that fails its own checks does not.
